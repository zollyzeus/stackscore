# StackScore Demo — Voiceover Script

**Accent:** Male, Indian English (confident, measured pace)  
**Total target duration:** ~90 seconds  
**TTS tools:** ElevenLabs / Murf.ai / Play.ht — use "Arjun" or "Rajan" voice preset  

---

## Scene 1 — Landing Page (0:00 – 0:12)

> "Most hiring platforms tell you if a candidate can code.  
> StackScore tells you if they can think.  
> It is the first AI-powered engineering assessment that scores not just code quality —  
> but design thinking, adaptability, and AI collaboration — all in one number."

---

## Scene 2 — The Eleven Axes (0:12 – 0:22)

> "Every submission is evaluated across eleven axes —  
> five design thinking axes covering problem clarity, architecture, tradeoffs, scalability, and security —  
> and six code quality axes covering correctness, efficiency, modularity, testing, observability, and AI usage."

---

## Scene 3 — Score Card Preview (0:22 – 0:30)

> "The result is a StackScore from zero to one hundred,  
> with a design sub-score, a code sub-score,  
> and a radar chart that maps every individual axis — at a glance."

---

## Scene 4 — Comparison Table (0:30 – 0:38)

> "Unlike HackerRank, LeetCode, or Codility,  
> StackScore is not a pass-fail algorithm test.  
> It evaluates how a candidate handles ambiguity, designs under constraints,  
> and adapts when requirements change mid-flight."

---

## Scene 5 — /why Page (0:38 – 0:44)

> "Real engineering work is never an isolated coding puzzle.  
> StackScore mirrors that reality — with a five-stage assessment  
> that takes a candidate from ambiguity all the way through debugging a defect in their own system."

---

## Scene 6 — Assessment Start (0:44 – 0:52)

> "Starting is simple. Choose your experience level — Junior, Mid, or Senior —  
> enter your name and email, and hit Start.  
> A fresh problem is generated by Claude, calibrated to your level."

---

## Scene 7 — Clarify Stage (0:52 – 1:02)

> "Stage one: Clarify.  
> The candidate converses with an AI stakeholder to scope the problem  
> before committing to a design.  
> On desktop, the problem brief stays pinned in a sidebar throughout the assessment."

---

## Scene 8 — Hints and Timer (1:02 – 1:08)

> "Candidates can toggle contextual hints for each stage,  
> and the overall sixty-minute timer is always visible.  
> Inactivity is monitored — progress is auto-saved and can be resumed."

---

## Scene 9 — Build Stage (1:08 – 1:16)

> "Stage three: Build — write a working implementation.  
> The in-browser runner executes JavaScript live.  
> On mobile, the brief collapses into a banner candidates can open at any time."

---

## Scene 10 — Score Card (1:16 – 1:28)

> "After submission, an AI judge with extended reasoning  
> evaluates every axis and generates a verifiable score card.  
> Per-axis feedback tells candidates exactly where they excelled and where to improve.  
> The score card carries a shareable link that hiring teams can verify as authentic."

---

## Scene 11 — Dashboard and Panel (1:28 – 1:38)

> "Hiring teams get a live dashboard — shortlist, hold, or reject candidates, add notes, and export to CSV.  
> Panelists can create custom assessments with their own brief,  
> assign them to specific candidates with an expiry date,  
> and receive webhook notifications the moment a submission is complete."

---

## Scene 12 — Compare and History (1:38 – 1:46)

> "Side-by-side radar comparison lets teams evaluate two candidates on the same axes at a glance.  
> Candidates track their own improvement trajectory over multiple attempts."

---

## Scene 13 — Closing on Landing (1:46 – 1:55)

> "StackScore is the full picture of an engineer — in one number.  
> Not just what they can code.  
> But how they think, how they design, and how they adapt.  
> Try it free at stackscore dot anandrajendran dot dev."
